\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report addresses the estimation of the position and velocity of
a racing vehicle on an asymmetric teardrop track from noisy
range-only beacon measurements. A bound-respecting nonlinear vehicle
motion model with smooth $C^{\infty}$ saturations is used as the
truth simulator; an optimised five-beacon GDOP-minimising layout
attenuates ranging noise across the entire track. An Extended Kalman
Filter is implemented with prediction-step Jacobians obtained from
CasADi automatic differentiation at every step, ensuring the
predictor and the simulator share identical dynamics. Across $100$
Monte-Carlo trials the filter achieves a mean arc-length error of
$0.226$~m, approximately $6.1$ times smaller than the
single-measurement GDOP benchmark, and passes a Normalized Innovation
Squared consistency check (empirical mean $4.994$, $95\%$ of samples
in the $95\%$ confidence band). A Moving Horizon Estimator with the
same nonlinear constraint exceeds the $10$~ms sampling budget by a
factor of $128$, supporting the EKF as the preferred estimator for
this implementation. A $9$-state augmented EKF recovers injected
sensor biases up to $0.8$~m to within $0.04$~m. Source code and
\code{results\_summary.json} are available at
\href{https://github.com/cuichenhui9-eng/ELE8101-CW2}{\texttt{github.com/cuichenhui9-eng/ELE8101-CW2}}.
\end{abstract}

\section{System Modelling}
\label{sec:modelling}

\subsection{Track Geometry}
\label{sec:geometry}
The track has an asymmetric teardrop shape comprising two circular arcs
connected by two inclined common external tangent straights. Arc~$A$
(large) has centre $A$ and centreline radius $R_A=(R+r)/2=48$~m;
arc~$B$ (small) has centre $B$ at distance $d=100$~m from~$A$ and
centreline radius $R_B=\rho+w=22$~m, where the track half-width
$w=(R-r)/2=2$~m and $\rho=20$~m is the inner-wall radius at~$B$ from
Figure~1 of the problem statement. The straights are inclined at
$\alpha=\arcsin\bigl((R_A-R_B)/d\bigr)=15.0701^{\circ}$, giving the
segment lengths
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
l_{\text{tan}}   &= \sqrt{d^2-(R_A-R_B)^2} &&= 96.5609~\text{m},\\
l_{\text{arc},A} &= R_A(\pi+2\alpha)       &&= 176.0466~\text{m},\\
l_{\text{arc},B} &= R_B(\pi-2\alpha)       &&= 57.5421~\text{m},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:seglen}
\end{equation}
and a total centreline perimeter
$L=2l_{\text{tan}}+l_{\text{arc},A}+l_{\text{arc},B}=426.7104$~m.

The vehicle position is parameterised by arc-length $s\in[0,L)$
(clockwise from the upper straight) and signed lateral offset
$\ell\in[-2,+2]$~m ($\ell>0$: outward); three piecewise-analytic
functions $p(s)$, $n(s)$ and $p(s,\ell)=p(s)+\ell\,n(s)$ implement
the geometry. Five verification tests pass: the track width is
$4.0000$~m everywhere, unit-normal magnitudes are $1$ to six
decimals, the arc--straight join gaps are below the $2$~mm probe
resolution, the shoelace area is negative ($-11\,617.32$~m$^2$,
confirming clockwise traversal), and $\Delta x=+4.83$~m at $s=0$.
The geometry is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:track}.

\subsection{Vehicle Motion Model}
\label{sec:motion}
The state vector is $z=[s,\ell,v_s,v_\ell]^{\!\top}$, where $v_s$ and
$v_\ell$ denote the longitudinal and lateral velocities. The
discrete-time nonlinear state equation is
\begin{equation}
z_{t+1}=f(z_t,w_t),\qquad w_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q),
\label{eq:dyn}
\end{equation}
with sampling time $h=0.01$~s ($100$~Hz, fixed by the problem
statement).

\paragraph{Bound-respecting nonlinear dynamics.}
Rather than enforcing the physical constraints $|\ell|<2$~m,
$|v_\ell|\le0.55$~m/s, $v_s\in(0,25]$~m/s by post-hoc clipping, the
model enforces them \emph{by construction} through three smooth
$C^{\infty}$ mechanisms: (i) a position-dependent stiffness
$k(\ell)=k_2\bigl(1+\beta(\ell^2/(\ell_{\max}^2-\ell^2))^2\bigr)$
that diverges as $|\ell|\to\ell_{\max}=2$~m, modelling the track walls
as elastic barriers; (ii) a $\tanh$ saturation keeping $|v_\ell|\le
0.55$~m/s, modelling tyre-friction and driver limits; and (iii) a
$\tanh$ saturation keeping $v_s\in(0,25]$~m/s, modelling engine/brake
capability. At the nominal operating point ($\ell=0$, $v_\ell=0$) the
dynamics reduce to the familiar linear spring--damper form, so normal
operation is indistinguishable from a linear Kalman filter; the
nonlinear terms activate only near the physical bounds. Because all
saturations are $C^{\infty}$, the dynamics remain differentiable
everywhere, preserving the validity of the EKF linearisation used in
Section~\ref{sec:ekf}.

\paragraph{Linearisation at the nominal operating point.}
At the nominal operating point ($\ell=0$, $v_\ell=0$) the
bound-respecting dynamics reduce to the closed-form linearisation
\begin{equation}
A_0=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & h & 0\\
0 & 1 & 0 & h\\
0 & 0 & \phi_s & 0\\
0 & -hk_2 & 0 & \phi_1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
G=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&0\\1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:A0}
\end{equation}
with $\phi_s=0.999$, $\phi_1=0.9$, $k_2=10$. The eigenvalues
$\{0.9113,0.9887,1,0.9601\}$ all satisfy $|\lambda|\le1$; the unit
eigenvalue corresponds to the integrator on $s$, the remaining modes
are strictly stable. $A_0$ is used here only for the Lyapunov
steady-state cross-check of Section~\ref{sec:bias}; the EKF
prediction step itself uses the local Jacobian
$A_t=\partial f/\partial z|_{\hat{z}_{t|t}}$ at every step, as
detailed in Section~\ref{sec:ekf-formulation}.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
Process noise $Q=\mathrm{diag}(0.5,0.002)$, justified by the
Monte-Carlo scans of Section~\ref{sec:noise}; measurement noise
$R=\sigma_y^2=1.5^2=2.25$~m$^2$; initial covariance
$P_0=\mathrm{diag}(100,1,25,0.5)$; initial state
$z_0=[0,0,10,0]^{\!\top}$.

\paragraph{Monte-Carlo verification.}
A Monte-Carlo study ($100$ trials, $3000$ steps each) with the full
nonlinear dynamics verifies all physical constraints with substantial
margin (Table~\ref{tab:mc}); no post-hoc clipping is applied.

\subsection{Beacon Placement Optimisation}
\label{sec:beacon}

\paragraph{GDOP criterion.}
Beacon-layout quality is quantified by the Geometric Dilution of
Precision~\cite{Langley1999}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{GDOP}(p)=\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}\bigl[(H(p)^{\!\top}H(p))^{-1}\bigr]},
\qquad
H_i(p)=\frac{p-a_i}{\|p-a_i\|},
\label{eq:gdop}
\end{equation}
so that the expected position error equals
$\mathrm{GDOP}\times\sigma_y$ and $\mathrm{GDOP}<1$ \emph{attenuates}
sensor noise. The selection criterion is the smallest beacon count
for which $\mathrm{GDOP}(p)<1$ at every sampled track position.

\paragraph{Connection to estimation theory.}
Following the BLUE development in the course~\cite{KalmanV}, for
distance measurements $y_i=\|p-a_i\|+v_i$ with
$v_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_y^2)$ the Fisher information matrix at
position $p$ is $I(p)=H(p)^{\!\top}H(p)/\sigma_y^2$, and the
Cram\'er--Rao lower bound satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tr}\bigl[\mathrm{CRLB}(p)\bigr]
=\sigma_y^2\,\mathrm{tr}\bigl[(H^{\!\top}H)^{-1}\bigr]
=\sigma_y^2\,\mathrm{GDOP}(p)^2.
\label{eq:crlb}
\end{equation}
Thus GDOP is the geometry-only scalar summary of the Fisher
information available from the beacon layout. An unbiased efficient
estimator cannot achieve position RMSE below
$\sigma_y\cdot\overline{\mathrm{GDOP}}=1.379$~m on average over the
track, setting a lower bound against which the EKF in
Section~\ref{sec:ekf} is benchmarked.

\paragraph{Algorithm and beacon count.}
Candidates are drawn from a $20\times16$ grid restricted to
$[w+5,w+30]$~m from the track boundary; the outer limit of $30$~m is
the Pareto-optimal knee identified by the spatial ablation of
Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}. A greedy algorithm with exhaustive
two-beacon initialisation and sequential single-beacon additions is
used as a tractable heuristic motivated by standard submodular
sensor-selection methods~\cite{Nemhauser1978}; submodularity of the
GDOP objective is not proved here, so the layout is validated
empirically by a swap refinement pass (which yields only a $1.4\%$
improvement, indicating proximity to a local optimum), a beacon-count
ablation, a single-beacon failure analysis, and an
installation-distance sensitivity study. Table~\ref{tab:gdop} shows an
abrupt threshold at $N=5$: with $N=4$, $0\%$ of track positions
satisfy $\mathrm{GDOP}<1$; with $N=5$, $100\%$ do. The five selected
coordinates (in~m) are $(-21.1,73.3)$, $(121.1,46.7)$,
$(26.3,-60.0)$, $(-52.6,20.0)$, $(89.5,-46.7)$; see
Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}.

\paragraph{Single-beacon failure robustness.}
Removing any one of the five beacons raises the mean GDOP from
$0.9193$ to between $1.048$ and $1.087$ ($+13.9$--$18.3\%$, worst
case $a_1$); coverage drops from $100\%$ to $0\%$ but mean GDOP
remains below $1.09$. The $N=5$ configuration is therefore selected
because it nominally meets the $\mathrm{GDOP}<1$ criterion and fails
gracefully under any single-point fault.

\paragraph{Installation boundary (spatial ablation).}
The $30$~m outer limit is data-derived (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}).
At $20$~m, only $88\%$ of the track achieves $\mathrm{GDOP}<1$; at
$30$~m coverage reaches $100\%$ with the largest single gain
($+2.9\%$); beyond $30$~m improvement falls below $0.5\%$. Minor
non-monotonicity at $60$~m and $100$~m is a greedy-search artefact
(different initial pairs are selected as the candidate pool grows).
The $30$~m boundary is therefore Pareto-optimal: the smallest outer
limit that meets the GDOP criterion everywhere.

Installation errors are stochastic in practice (GPS accuracy,

mean-GDOP $0.9205$ ($+0.1\%$), $95$th percentile $0.9308$

(Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}). The design is therefore robust to

\subsection{Sensor Bias Handling via State Augmentation}
\label{sec:bias}
Each beacon may exhibit a constant additive measurement bias $\beta_i$
due to hardware calibration errors or multipath effects. If ignored,
the EKF estimate drifts persistently. Following the
state-augmentation approach of~\cite{KalmanVI} (adapted from dynamics
bias to measurement bias) and~\cite{Simon2006} (Ch.~13), the state
is augmented to $z_\text{aug}=[s,\ell,v_s,v_\ell,\beta_1,\dots,\beta_5]
^{\!\top}\in\mathbb{R}^9$, with dynamics
$z_{\text{aug},t+1}=A_\text{aug}z_{\text{aug},t}+G_\text{aug}
w_{\text{aug},t}$ and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_\text{aug}&=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&I_5\end{bmatrix},\quad
G_\text{aug}=\begin{bmatrix}G&0\\0&I_5\end{bmatrix},\\[2pt]
Q_\text{aug}&=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(0.5,\;0.002,\;
\underbrace{10^{-6},\dots,10^{-6}}_{5}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug}
\end{equation}
The $I_5$ block in $A_\text{aug}$ models each bias as a random walk.
The $I_5$ block in $G_\text{aug}$ assigns each beacon an
\emph{independent} noise channel: the five hardware units have
uncorrelated internal drifts, and a shared channel would incorrectly
imply $100\%$ correlation. Initial bias uncertainty is
$P_{0,\beta_i}=25$~m$^2$ ($\sigma_\beta=5$~m). The augmented measurement
function is $h_i(z_\text{aug})=\|p(s,\ell)-a_i\|+\beta_i$, with bias
Jacobian $\partial h_i/\partial \beta_j=\delta_{ij}$ (identity block
$I_5$). Numerical verification with known biases recovered each
bias to within $6\times10^{-15}$~m.

\paragraph{Static observability and practical bias estimation.}
The frozen-linearisation observability matrix
$\mathcal{O}=[C;\,CA_\text{aug};\,\dots;\,CA_\text{aug}^{\,8}]$
satisfies $\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{O})=8$, one short of the state
dimension. Two structural causes contribute: $C$ has zero columns for
$v_s$ and $v_\ell$ (range does not depend on velocity directly), and
the dynamic coupling $CA_\text{aug}$ adds only one further
independent direction through the position--velocity integrator. This
static test therefore does \emph{not} prove full joint observability
of the vehicle state and all five biases at any single instant, and
the augmented filter is not claimed to be globally observable from a
single linearisation. The argument is instead the weaker but more
honest one: $v_\ell$ is recovered by the EKF through the dynamic
coupling ($Ce_{v_\ell}=0$ but $CA_\text{aug}e_{v_\ell}\ne0$), and the
augmented filter's practical bias-estimation ability is verified
empirically over a moving trajectory with injected biases in
Section~\ref{sec:bias-aug}~\cite{Hautus1969,Simon2006}.

\paragraph{Steady-state cross-check.}
Following~\cite{KalmanI}, for the strictly stable
$[\ell,v_s,v_\ell]$ sub-system the covariance converges to the
unique stationary value $P_\infty$ satisfying the discrete-time
Lyapunov equation $P_\infty=A_\text{sub}P_\infty A_\text{sub}^{\!
\top}+G_\text{sub}QG_\text{sub}^{\!\top}$, giving
$\sigma_{\ell,\infty}=0.032$~m, $\sigma_{v_\ell,\infty}=0.103$~m/s,
and a mean-reversion standard deviation $\sigma_{v_s,\infty}=
2.53$~m/s about the target $v_s=10$~m/s. The $3\sigma$ envelopes
($0.095$~m and $0.309$~m/s) agree with the nonlinear Monte-Carlo
maxima ($0.109$~m and $0.335$~m/s) to within the expected tail
factor, confirming that $A(\hat{z})$ is a faithful local
linearisation at the nominal operating point --- which is precisely
what the EKF relies on at every prediction step. The tanh saturation
provides additional protection against off-nominal excursions that
the linear model cannot rule out a priori
(Figure~\ref{fig:stress}).

\section{Extended Kalman Filter}
\label{sec:ekf}

The estimator chapters that follow build directly on the modelling
component of Section~\ref{sec:modelling}: the linearised dynamics
matrices $A_0$, $G$ at the nominal operating point and the
process/measurement noise covariances $Q=\mathrm{diag}(0.5, 0.002)$
and $R=2.25\,\mathrm{m}^2 \cdot I_5$ (Section~\ref{sec:motion}); the
five GDOP-optimised beacon coordinates and the resulting
Cram\'er--Rao lower bound
$\sigma_y\cdot\overline{\mathrm{GDOP}}=1.379$~m on single-shot
position RMSE (Section~\ref{sec:beacon}); the Lyapunov steady-state
standard deviations $\sigma_{\ell,\infty}=0.032$~m,
$\sigma_{v_\ell,\infty}=0.103$~m/s, $\sigma_{v_s,\infty}=2.53$~m/s
used here as theoretical references (Section~\ref{sec:bias}); the
augmented-state formulation
$z_\text{aug}=[s,\ell,v_s,v_\ell,\beta_1,\dots,\beta_5]^{\!\top}$ with its
noise-free $h_\text{aug}$ verification (Section~\ref{sec:bias},
implemented numerically in Section~\ref{sec:bias-aug}); and the
full nonlinear truth model $f(\cdot)$ of Section~\ref{sec:motion},
used in the near-boundary stress test of
Section~\ref{sec:bdry}.

\subsection{EKF Formulation}
\label{sec:ekf-formulation}

Both the state transition $f(\cdot)$ from Section~\ref{sec:motion} and
the range-measurement function $h(\cdot)$ are genuinely nonlinear: $f$
contains the position-dependent stiffness $k(\ell)$ and the smooth
$\tanh$ saturations on $v_s$ and $v_\ell$, while $h$ returns the
Euclidean distance from the vehicle to each of the five anchors
through the piecewise track geometry of Section~\ref{sec:geometry}.
The standard Kalman filter is therefore insufficient; an EKF that
linearises both $f$ and $h$ at the current estimate is required, in
exact correspondence with the nonlinear branch of the lecturer's
notebook~\cite{Sopasakis_5_ekf}.

Following the convention of~\cite{Sopasakis_5_ekf}, the measurement
update executed when a measurement $y_t$ is received is
\begin{align}
\nu_t          &= y_t - h(\hat{z}_{t|t-1}), \label{eq:innov}\\
S_t            &= C_t\,\Sigma_{t|t-1}\,C_t^{\!\top} + R,
                  \label{eq:cov}\\
\hat{z}_{t|t}  &= \hat{z}_{t|t-1} + \Sigma_{t|t-1} C_t^{\!\top} S_t^{-1}
                  \nu_t, \label{eq:meas}\\
\Sigma_{t|t}   &= \Sigma_{t|t-1} - \Sigma_{t|t-1} C_t^{\!\top} S_t^{-1}
                  C_t\,\Sigma_{t|t-1}, \label{eq:sigm}
\end{align}
where $C_t=\partial h/\partial z|_{\hat{z}_{t|t-1}}$ is the
measurement Jacobian and
the additive-noise structure makes $J_v h\equiv I_5$, so the linearised innovation covariance reduces to $C_t\Sigma_{t|t-1}C_t^{\!\top}+R$ with no auxiliary rescaling. The time update
propagates the estimate forward through the \emph{full} nonlinear
dynamics
\begin{align}
\hat{z}_{t+1|t}  &= f(\hat{z}_{t|t},\,0), \label{eq:tpred}\\
\Sigma_{t+1|t}   &= A_t\,\Sigma_{t|t}\,A_t^{\!\top}
                    + L_t\,Q\,L_t^{\!\top}, \label{eq:tsigm}
\end{align}
with the prediction-step Jacobians
$A_t=\partial f/\partial z|_{\hat{z}_{t|t}}$ and
$L_t=\partial f/\partial w|_{\hat{z}_{t|t}}$ both evaluated at the
current state estimate at every time step.
The state-input Jacobian $A_t$ accounts for the local effect of the
nonlinear stiffness $k(\ell)$ when the vehicle is near a boundary; the
noise-input Jacobian $L_t$ propagates process-noise covariance through
the same $\tanh$-saturated channels that the truth model itself uses,
so the predictor and the simulator have identical noise gains by
construction. The two Jacobians coincide with the closed-form
$A_0$ and $G$ of Section~\ref{sec:motion} only at the nominal operating
point; everywhere else they differ, and using the local Jacobians is
what removes the predictor--simulator mismatch that an
$A_0$-frozen prediction would carry. All four Jacobians
($\partial h/\partial z$, $\partial h/\partial v$, $\partial f/\partial
z$, $\partial f/\partial w$) are obtained from CasADi
automatic differentiation~\cite{Andersson2019}; the measurement
function is built piecewise-symbolically via \code{cs.if\_else} so
that the four track segments (two straights and two arcs) of
Section~\ref{sec:geometry} are handled exactly across segment
transitions, with no finite-difference discretisation error.
The variable names (\code{x\_t\_pred}, \code{sigma\_t\_pred},
\code{x\_t\_meas}, \code{sigma\_t\_meas}) and numbered loop comments
follow the lecturer's convention so that the implementation is
auditable against the lecture material. The setup layer of
\code{Estimator.ipynb} configures the track geometry, anchor
coordinates, the noise covariances $Q$ and $R$, the CasADi symbolic
functions $h$, $j_{hx}$, $j_{hv}$, $j_{fx}$, $j_{fw}$, $f_{\mathrm{
fun}}$, and the EKF primitives \code{measurement\_update} and
\code{time\_update}. All quantities are imported from the modelling
component (Section~\ref{sec:modelling}); the layer is reused by every
subsequent cell without modification.

\subsection{Internal Model Alignment}
\label{sec:internal-model}

The longitudinal velocity dynamics specified by the simulator are
autoregressive of the first order, centred at the nominal speed
$v_{s,\mathrm{tgt}}=10$~m/s rather than zero,
\begin{equation}
v_{s,t+1}=\phi_s\,v_{s,t}+(1-\phi_s)\,v_{s,\mathrm{tgt}}+w_{s,t}.
\label{eq:vsdyn}
\end{equation}
The constant term $(1-\phi_s)\,v_{s,\mathrm{tgt}}$ in
\eqref{eq:vsdyn} would not appear in any \emph{linear} time-update
that uses only the state-transition matrix $A_0$. An early development
iteration that began from such a linear $A_0$-only predictor exhibited
a small but persistent bias in the longitudinal velocity estimate;
inspection of the mean velocity trajectories produced by the simulator
versus the predictor isolated the missing affine term as the cause.
The current implementation removes the issue by construction:
because the predictor uses the full nonlinear function
$\hat{z}_{t+1|t}=f(\hat{z}_{t|t},0)$ from~\eqref{eq:tpred} rather than
$A_0\hat{z}_{t|t}$, the affine mean-reversion term in
\eqref{eq:vsdyn} is part of $f$ itself and propagates without any
explicit correction. The same logic applies to the lateral
restoring force $-h\,k(\ell)\ell$ and the $\tanh$ saturations: all are
contained inside the single function call $f(\cdot,0)$ that the
predictor evaluates, so the EKF's internal model is by definition
identical to the simulator's truth dynamics. The NIS consistency
check in Section~\ref{sec:nis} confirms statistically that no residual
internal-model bias remains.

\subsection{Single-Run Simulation}
\label{sec:single-run}

A representative single-lap simulation was conducted over $N=4300$ time
steps (approximately $43$~s at the nominal speed). The initial state
is $z_0=[0,0,10,0]^{\!\top}$ with deliberately diffuse initial
covariance
\begin{equation}
P_0=\mathrm{diag}(100,1,25,0.5)
   =\mathrm{diag}(10^2,\,1^2,\,5^2,\,0.707^2),
\label{eq:p0}
\end{equation}
which corresponds to standard-deviation priors of approximately
$10$~m on arc-length (about $1$~s of travel at the nominal
$10$~m/s), $1$~m on lateral offset (one half-lane-width), $5$~m/s on
longitudinal velocity (50\% of the nominal speed), and $0.707$~m/s on
lateral velocity (slightly above the $0.55$~m/s physical bound). The
diffuseness is deliberate: the recursive filter forgets its prior
within several hundred steps, as the closed-loop covariance trace of
Figure~\ref{fig:p-conv} (Section~\ref{sec:single-run}) and the
$P_0$-sensitivity scan of Table~\ref{tab:p0-scan} both confirm.

The remaining design parameters use ``hard'' problem-statement
quantities wherever possible:
$h=1/100=0.01$~s is fixed by the $100$~Hz beacon update rate, and
$R=\sigma_y^2 I_5=1.5^2 I_5=2.25\,I_5\,\mathrm{m}^2$ uses the
problem-statement range standard deviation $\sigma_y=1.5$~m. The
process-noise covariance $Q=\mathrm{diag}(0.5,0.002)$ is not
specified by the problem and was selected by the modelling-side
parameter scans of Section~\ref{sec:noise}; the NIS consistency
check in Section~\ref{sec:nis} below provides an independent
estimator-side confirmation that this $Q$ matches the true noise
statistics to within $0.1\%$ of the theoretical NIS mean. Results are
shown in Figure~\ref{fig:cell2}.

The diffuse-prior values $P_0=\mathrm{diag}(100,1,25,0.5)$ are an

filter forgets its initial covariance after a few hundred steps
regardless of how diffuse or tight the prior is, so the choice
affects only the warm-up duration and not steady-state accuracy.

\subsection{NIS Consistency Check}
\label{sec:nis}

Performance metrics such as RMSE confirm that the filter is accurate
\emph{in this run}, but they do not directly verify that the design
covariances $Q$ and $R$ match the true noise statistics. A filter could
achieve a low RMSE while being internally miscalibrated --- over- or
under-confident in its own uncertainty --- and the miscalibration would
only surface under stress. The standard diagnostic for filter
consistency~\cite{Sopasakis_6_tutorial} is the Normalized Innovation
Squared (NIS) test:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\nu_t          &= y_t - h(\hat{z}_{t|t-1}),\\
S_t            &= C_t\,\Sigma_{t|t-1}\,C_t^{\!\top}+R,\\
\mathrm{NIS}_t &= \nu_t^{\!\top}\,S_t^{-1}\,\nu_t.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nis}
\end{equation}
If the filter is correctly tuned, $\mathrm{NIS}_t$ follows a chi-square
distribution with $n_y=5$ degrees of freedom (the measurement
dimension), giving expected mean $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{NIS}]=5$ and a
$95\%$ confidence interval of $[0.83, 12.83]$.

This estimator-side consistency check is independent of and
complementary to the simulator-side scan of Section~\ref{sec:noise}:
the two analyses approach the same $Q$-matrix selection from opposite
directions, and agreement between them (shown below) provides
stronger evidence than either could on its own.

NIS samples were collected from all $100$ Monte-Carlo trials
(excluding the warm-up window), giving approximately $380{,}000$
samples in total. The empirical distribution is compared against the
theoretical chi-square pdf in Figure~\ref{fig:nis}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{B2_nis_check.png}
\caption{NIS consistency check across the $100$-trial Monte-Carlo
dataset: empirical histogram against the theoretical $\chi^2(5)$ pdf.
Empirical mean $4.994$ vs.\ theoretical $5.00$; $95.0\%$ of samples
fall within the $95\%$ confidence band $[0.83,12.83]$.}
\label{fig:nis}
\end{figure}

The agreement is close to the theoretical value: empirical mean $4.994$ versus
theoretical $5.00$ (relative error $0.1\%$), and $95.0\%$ of samples
within the $95\%$ confidence band. The histogram shape is visually
indistinguishable from the chi-square pdf across the entire range.
The interpretation is that the filter is statistically consistent: the
design covariances $Q$ and $R$ match the true noise statistics imposed
by the simulator. Crucially, this provides the desired estimator-side
independent confirmation of the noise-parameter selection: two
independent analyses (simulator-side constraint preservation in
Section~\ref{sec:noise} and the present estimator-side innovation
statistics) both select $Q=\mathrm{diag}(0.5,0.002)$, strengthening
confidence in the result.

\subsection{Near-Boundary Stress Test}
\label{sec:bdry}

Section~\ref{sec:motion} includes an adversarial stress test at the
simulator level: the bound-respecting nonlinear dynamics is integrated
from three initial conditions to verify that the simulator's nonlinear
spring keeps $|\ell|<2$~m in all cases. That test answers the question
\emph{``does the simulated vehicle stay on the track?''} It does not
answer the complementary question relevant for the estimator:
\emph{``does the EKF still track the vehicle accurately when the
vehicle operates near the boundary?''} The latter matters because the
position-dependent stiffness $k(\ell)$ diverges as $|\ell|\to2$~m,
so the EKF's linear prediction step (which uses $A_0$ at the nominal
point) is most stressed in this regime.

The present section provides the estimator-side counterpart to the
simulator-side stress test, completing a closed-loop validation:
Section~\ref{sec:motion} confirms the dynamics keeps the vehicle on
track; this section confirms the EKF can find the vehicle there.
The truth model used in this experiment is the full nonlinear
$f(\cdot)$ of Section~\ref{sec:motion} (smooth $C^{\infty}$
saturations, no post-hoc clipping), guaranteeing consistency between
the modelling component and the simulator code used here.

The conditions used here differ from the adversarial impulse of
Section~\ref{sec:motion} ($\ell_0=1.5$, $v_{\ell,0}=2.0$~m/s)
because the two tests have different goals. The
modelling-side test stresses the dynamics with a $v_\ell$ nearly four
times the physical $0.55$~m/s bound, to verify that the nonlinear
spring activates in unrealistic conditions. The present test stresses
the EKF under realistic but extreme operating conditions: vehicle
initialised at $\ell_0=\pm1.8$~m (within $0.2$~m of the boundary)
with $v_{\ell,0}=\pm0.3$~m/s (within the operational bound). Three
scenarios are run, each one full lap with identical seed: A nominal
($\ell_0=0$, $v_{\ell,0}=0$); B near $+$bound; C symmetric near
$-$bound. Results are in Table~\ref{tab:bdy} and
Figure~\ref{fig:cell7}.

\section{Comparison with Moving Horizon Estimation}
\label{sec:mhe}

A complete Moving Horizon Estimator (MHE) implementation is now
presented alongside the associated real-time-feasibility analysis,
establishing the EKF as the preferred estimator for this implementation for the
problem-statement $100$~Hz real-time requirement.

The MHE is implemented following the Bayesian full-information
estimation framework presented in the course lecture
notebook~\cite{Sopasakis_2_bayesian_fie}, which solves a constrained
optimisation problem at each time step, minimising the Bayesian
negative log-likelihood over a sliding window of $M$ most recent
measurements,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\bm{x}_{0:M},\,\bm{w}_{0:M-1}}\quad
&\ell_{x_0}(x_0)
 +\sum_{\tau=0}^{M-1}\ell_w(w_\tau)
 +\sum_{\tau=0}^{M}\ell_v\!\bigl(y_\tau-h(x_\tau)\bigr),\\[2pt]
\text{s.t.}\quad
&x_{\tau+1}=f(x_\tau,w_\tau),\quad
 \ell\in[-2,2]~\text{m},\;v_s\in[0,25]~\text{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mhe}
\end{equation}
The dynamics constraint is the same nonlinear $f(\cdot)$ used by
the EKF prediction step in \eqref{eq:tpred}. The three cost components $\ell_{x_0}$, $\ell_w$,
$\ell_v$ correspond to the arrival cost, process-noise penalty and
measurement-residual penalty used in the course notebook. The MHE and
the EKF therefore share the identical truth model and identical
prediction dynamics; they differ only in the solver
(constrained-optimisation versus closed-form Kalman update) and the
horizon (sliding window of $M$ vs. single time step).

The optimisation was solved using SLSQP from
SciPy~\cite{Virtanen2020} with horizon $M=10$, giving a decision
variable of dimension $64$ and $40$ equality constraints. The choice
$Mh=0.10$~s corresponds to roughly $1$~m of vehicle motion at the
nominal $10$~m/s, a natural scale for a finite-lookback arc-length
estimator and a deliberately short window: SLSQP cost grows
super-linearly in $M$, so a longer horizon would only widen the
real-time gap reported below. If even this short horizon is
infeasible, no longer one is. The arrival cost uses the previous MHE
output as mean with a fixed diagonal prior covariance
(self-bootstrapping). The constraint Jacobian
$\partial[x_{\tau+1}-f(x_\tau,w_\tau)]/\partial(x,w)$ is constructed
once symbolically with CasADi and passed to SLSQP analytically rather
than recomputed by finite differences at every solver iteration; this
is essential for both speed and reliability under the nonlinear
constraint. Results are shown in Table~\ref{tab:mhe} and
Figure~\ref{fig:cell4}.

The MHE achieves comparable arc-length accuracy but markedly worse
lateral accuracy ($0.229$~m, an order of magnitude larger than the

$1275$~ms exceeds the $10$~ms sampling budget by a factor of $128$,

this hardware. The EKF requires only matrix multiplications, a
$5\times5$ linear solve, and four CasADi-evaluated Jacobians per step,
completing in $0.43$~ms (Section~\ref{sec:complexity}). On these
grounds the EKF is selected as the primary estimator. MHE could
become competitive with a problem-specific QP solver exploiting the
banded horizon structure, but this is outside the scope of the
current work. The conclusion is that, for this problem, the
structural advantage MHE offers (hard bound enforcement) is already
provided implicitly by the bound-respecting dynamics of
Section~\ref{sec:motion}, so MHE's theoretical benefit does not
translate to measurable improvement here.

\section{Bias-Augmented EKF: Implementation and Verification}
\label{sec:bias-aug}

The problem statement requires a discussion of how the estimator
handles sensor bias. Section~\ref{sec:bias} derives the theoretical
formulation: an augmented state
$z_\text{aug}=[s,\ell,v_s,v_\ell,\beta_1,\dots,\beta_5]^{\!\top}\in
\mathbb{R}^9$ with block-diagonal $A_\text{aug}$, $G_\text{aug}$,
$Q_\text{aug}$ matrices, following the state-augmentation approach of
the course lecture notebook~\cite{Sopasakis_4_kalman_variants} and
\cite{Simon2006}~(Ch.~13). The detectability analysis
($\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{O})=8$ with
$e_{v_\ell}\notin\mathrm{null}(\mathcal{O})$) and the noise-free
measurement-function verification (recovery to within
$6\times10^{-15}$~m) are also developed in Section~\ref{sec:bias}.

The present section provides the corresponding noise-domain
verification: the $9$-state augmented EKF is implemented and run on
the simulator output with realistic measurement noise
($\sigma_y=1.5$~m) and injected biases.
Section~\ref{sec:bias} establishes mathematical correctness; this
section establishes engineering viability under noise.

The augmented measurement function is
\begin{equation}
h_{\text{aug},i}(z_\text{aug})
=\bigl\|p(s,\ell)-a_i\bigr\|_2+\beta_i+v_i,
\qquad i=1,\dots,5,
\label{eq:haug}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ is the known beacon position and $\beta_i$ is
the unknown bias to be estimated. All Jacobians
$\partial h_\text{aug}/\partial z_\text{aug}$ and
$\partial h_\text{aug}/\partial v$ are built symbolically in CasADi;
the bias sub-Jacobian $I_5$ emerges automatically from the additive
structure of \eqref{eq:haug}, with no hand coding required. The
augmented measurement-update and time-update functions follow the
same pattern as the baseline $4$-state primitives.

To verify the augmented filter under noise, a $43$~s single-lap
simulation was run with the bias vector
$[+0.5,-0.3,+0.2,+0.8,-0.4]$~m injected on the five beacons (the same
test vector used in Section~\ref{sec:bias} for the symbolic
$h_\text{aug}$ verification). Two filters were run in parallel on the
identical biased measurement stream: the $9$-state augmented EKF, and
the $4$-state EKF of Section~\ref{sec:ekf} (baseline that ignores
bias). Results are in Table~\ref{tab:bias} and
Figure~\ref{fig:cell5}.

\section{Computational Complexity and Real-time Feasibility}
\label{sec:complexity}

The computational cost of the EKF measurement update is dominated by
the product $C\,\Sigma\,C^{\!\top}$ and the linear solve of the
$5\times5$ innovation matrix $S$, which scale as $O(n_y^2 n_x)$ and
$O(n_y^3)$ respectively, with $n_x=4$ and $n_y=5$. The prediction step
adds two CasADi function evaluations per step (for $A_t$ and $L_t$)
and one matrix product $A_t\Sigma A_t^{\!\top}$. For the $9$-state
augmented EKF, the state-side cost rises modestly to
$O(n_y^2 n_\text{aug})$ with $n_\text{aug}=9$. Each operation
involves only small dense matrices and one CasADi call.

A direct \texttt{timeit} benchmark of the two EKF primitives over
$500$ repetitions in the setup-cell environment of
\code{Estimator.ipynb} gives mean per-call times of
$t_{\text{meas}}=0.20$~ms and $t_{\text{time}}=0.23$~ms, for a
combined per-step EKF runtime of $0.43$~ms. The sampling period is
$h=0.01$~s, giving a per-step budget of $10$~ms; the EKF therefore
uses $4.3\%$ of the budget, leaving more than an order of magnitude
of headroom for embedded deployment, additional sensor fusion, or the
$9$-state augmented variant. This benchmark is exported to
\code{results\_summary.json} so any drift between the reported
runtime and the implementation is detectable on every notebook
execution.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A vehicle state estimator for an asymmetric teardrop track was
designed and validated, combining a bound-respecting nonlinear
modelling component with a CasADi-based EKF implementation. The
modelling chapter formulated a smooth $C^{\infty}$ truth model that
enforces the lane-corridor and velocity constraints by structural
design rather than post-hoc clipping, derived a five-beacon
GDOP-minimising layout that attenuates ranging noise across $100\%$
of the track, validated the noise-parameter choices by Monte-Carlo
scan, and developed the state-augmentation formulation used for
sensor-bias handling. The estimator chapter implemented the EKF with
prediction-step Jacobians $A_t$ and $L_t$ obtained from CasADi
automatic differentiation at every step from the same dynamics that
serve as the truth simulator. Across $100$ Monte-Carlo trials the
filter achieved a mean arc-length error of $0.226$~m, approximately
$6.1\times$ smaller than the single-measurement GDOP benchmark; it
passed a Normalized Innovation Squared consistency check (empirical
mean $4.994$, $95\%$ of samples in the $95\%$ band); and its
closed-loop covariance trace decayed by approximately $5\times$ over
the warm-up window. A Moving Horizon Estimator with the same
nonlinear constraint exceeded the $10$~ms budget by $128\times$,
supporting the EKF as the preferred estimator. The $9$-state
augmented variant recovered injected beacon biases up to $0.8$~m to
within $0.04$~m, with sensitivity to the bias-drift variance
remaining below $0.12$~m across four decades. The complete EKF
pipeline executes in $0.43$~ms per step.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Opportunities for further work, not pursued here due to scope
constraints, include: (a) combining the EKF and MHE into a warm-start
hybrid where the EKF provides the arrival cost for a short-horizon MHE
invoked only near the track boundary; (b) replacing the SLSQP solver
with a code-generated QP solver tailored to the banded horizon
structure, which could close the real-time gap by one to two orders of
magnitude; (c) extending the bias model from a pure random walk to a
first-order Markov process, accommodating slowly varying drift
(e.g.\ thermal) in addition to constant offset.
